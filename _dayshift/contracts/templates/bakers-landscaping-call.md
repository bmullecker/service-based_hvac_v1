# Cold Call Script — Baker's Landscaping and Maintenance
**Prospect:** Efrium | Baker's Landscaping and Maintenance | York, PA
**Source:** Found via Facebook (tagged in a landscaping recommendation thread)
**Known gaps:** No website, no Google Business Profile, no Facebook business page, no logo

---

## OPENING

"Hey, is this Efrium? This is Bill Mullecker. I'm a web developer and designer local to York. I found your business on Facebook and noticed you don't have a website yet. I build affordable websites for local businesses. Do you have two minutes?"

**IF HE SAYS "WHO IS THIS" / SOUNDS SUSPICIOUS:**
"Totally fair — I'm not trying to sell you anything shady, I just build websites for local trades and service businesses here in York. Two minutes, that's it."

**IF HE SAYS "NOW'S NOT A GOOD TIME":**
"No worries at all — when's a better time to catch you? I can call back."
*(Get a specific day/time, write it in the sheet, hang up. Do NOT push further.)*

**IF HE SAYS "SURE" / "OKAY" → CONTINUE**

---

## DISCOVERY (understand his situation before pitching)

"Perfect, thanks. Quick question — where do most of your landscaping leads come from right now? Referrals, word of mouth, Google, Facebook?"

*(Let him answer. Actually listen — this tells you what to emphasize.)*

"Got it. And are you doing this full-time, or is it more of a side thing right now?"

*(This tells you how much he can realistically spend and how serious he is about growing.)*

---

## THE PROBLEM (connect what he told you to what's missing)

"So here's what I'm seeing — when someone in York searches 'landscaping near me' on Google, they're not going to find you, because you don't have a website or a Google Business Profile. That's leads going straight to your competitors who DO show up. Does that track with what you're seeing?"

*(Let him respond — validates the pain before you pitch the fix.)*

---

## THE OFFER (core pitch)

"Here's what I do. I build you a professional website. For a business your size, I'd do it for five hundred bucks — I usually charge seven fifty for that, but I'm taking on a few new clients right now, so you're getting the lower rate. Then fifty bucks a month covers your domain, hosting, and a professional email address, so instead of a Gmail you've got something like efrium@bakerslandscaping.com. That's the foundation."

*(Say the $750 → $500 line once, plainly, then move on. Don't over-explain the discount or dwell on it — that undercuts the value instead of reinforcing it.)*

*(Pause. Let it land. Don't rush into upsells yet.)*

"Sound like something you'd want?"

---

## IF HE WANTS MORE THAN ONE PAGE

"Sure — if you want more than just the one page, like a dedicated services page, an about page, that kind of thing, I can do a full multi-page site for you. Normally that runs twelve hundred to fifteen hundred, but since we're starting at a smaller-business rate, I'd do it for one thousand flat. That gets you a proper homepage, services page, about page, contact page, and a gallery — the whole setup."

*(Only offer this if HE brings up wanting more. Don't upsell into it unprompted — the single page is the right fit for his size, and you already noted that on the call.)*

---

## IF HE HESITATES ON PRICE (concession, not opener)

The $750 anchor is already stated in the core offer above — don't repeat it here. Just hold five hundred first. Only drop to four hundred if he pushes back or goes quiet on the number.

"Tell you what — since it's your first site with me, I'll bring it down to four hundred. That gets you a single page: info about your business, a contact form so people can reach you, and a gallery of your best work. That's everything you need to start pulling leads. Ready to move forward?"

*(Don't ask "does that sound like a good deal?" — it invites a soft no. Ask for the decision directly.)*

---

## IF HE SAYS YES → UPSELLS (offer, don't dump all at once)

"Awesome. A couple other things I noticed that'd help a lot —"

**Google Business Profile:**
"You don't have a Google Business Profile set up. That's the map listing that shows up when people search — reviews, hours, photos, all of it. I can set that up for a hundred bucks, one-time. That's honestly one of the highest-ROI things I can do for you."

**Facebook Business Page:**
"You're also not on Facebook as a business — just personal posts, right? I can build you a real business page, branded to match your site, for seventy-five bucks."

**Logo:**
"Last thing — do you have an actual logo right now, or just text?"
*(If no logo:)* "I can design you a clean, professional logo for a hundred fifty bucks. It'll be used across your website, Facebook, business cards, truck decals, whatever you need down the line."

*(After going through these, total it up so it's not a surprise:)*

"So altogether, if you wanted the whole package — website, Google Business Profile, Facebook page, and a logo — that's eight hundred twenty-five bucks upfront, then fifty a month for hosting and email. But we don't have to do it all at once. We can start with just the website if that's an easier entry point."

---

## OBJECTION HANDLING

**"That's more than I want to spend right now."**
"Totally get it. What if we just start with the website — five hundred bucks — and we add the Google Business Profile and logo later once you're seeing leads come in? No pressure to do it all today."

**"Let me think about it."**
"Of course, no rush. Can I ask — what's the main thing you want to think through? Price, timing, or just want to see examples of my work first?"
*(Listen to the actual objection, address it directly. If he really just needs time:)*
"Fair enough. I'll text you my info so you have it. When you're ready, just reach out — no pressure either way."

**"I don't have the money right now."**
"No worries at all. I'll keep your info and you can reach out whenever timing's better. In the meantime, feel free to follow me on Facebook — I post examples of what I build."

**"I already have someone / I'm working with someone else."**
"Oh nice, are they building you a full site, or just doing social media? If you ever want a second opinion or run into pricing that doesn't work, feel free to give me a call."

**"How do I know you're legit / not a scam?"**
"Totally fair question. I'm a sole proprietor here in York, I've got an EIN, and everything's done through a written contract — spells out exactly what you're getting, the price, the timeline, all of it. Nothing happens until you sign and agree to the terms."

**"Can you send me info instead of talking now?"**
"Yeah, absolutely. What's the best email or number to text you some info and a couple examples of sites I've built?"
*(Get contact info, move him to Prospects sheet with status "Sent Info — Follow Up.")*

**"How fast could you get this done?"**
"Once you sign and the deposit's in, I can usually have a site built and live within a week or two, depending on how quickly you get me your photos and info."

---

## CLOSING (if he's ready)

"Awesome — I can send the contract over today. Once you sign and send the deposit, I'll get started right away. What's the best email to send that to?"

*(Get email. Confirm payment method — Zelle or Cash App.)*

"Perfect, you'll have it in your inbox within the hour. Excited to work with you."

---

## IF HE SAYS NO TO THE WEBSITE — ONE FALLBACK MENU (say once, don't chain)

Don't reveal that GBP is actually your top-priority recommendation for him — that undercuts the website pitch if said too early. And don't re-pitch one item at a time after each "no" — that turns into "well, also this, also this" and starts to feel like begging.

Instead, say the whole fallback menu in one breath, then let him pick what's useful (or nothing):

"No worries on the full website. If it'd help, I can also just get you set up with a Google Business Profile — that's the map listing with your reviews and hours, a hundred bucks — or a Facebook business page for seventy-five. I can also manage both with a monthly post so they stay active and look current, that's fifty bucks a month on top. Whichever piece is actually useful to you, I can just do that one — no pressure to do it all."

*(Said once. If he picks one or more, close it like a normal sale — email, payment, next steps. If he says no to all of it, move straight to the flat-out no close below. Don't re-offer anything a second time.)*

---

## IF HE SAYS NO, FLAT OUT (to everything)

"No problem at all, I appreciate you hearing me out. Keep my number — if things change or you want to revisit it down the road, just give me a call. Have a good one."

*(Hang up. Log him in Pipeline/Prospects as "Not Interested" with a note on why. Move on to the next call.)*
