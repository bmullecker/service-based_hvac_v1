PART 1 — THE BUSINESS MODEL
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

You build high-converting websites for local small businesses with no web
presence — trades, restaurants, personal services — and package it with
hosting, recurring support, local SEO, print assets, and Google optimization.

You are not a freelancer who builds sites.
You are the marketing department they never had.

YOUR EDGE:
- You show up with a demo already built. No one else does that.
- You have a print partner for full identity packages.
- You are local. You can meet them. That matters to a 55-year-old plumber
  more than any portfolio.
- You speak their language. Not "conversion funnels." Just: get the phone
  to ring.


IDEAL CLIENT PROFILE:
- In business 10+ years (proven, not a startup risk)
- Word-of-mouth only — no ads, no website
- Owner-operated or small team (2-5 people)
- Estimated $200K-$1M annual revenue
- Trades: plumbers, HVAC, electricians, roofers, painters, landscapers
- Food: local restaurants, diners, cafes
- Personal services: salons, barbers, auto shops, dog groomers


HOW YOU FIND THEM:
1. Google Maps — search the trade/category in a town
2. Filter for: locally named businesses, no website link in the listing,
   reviews that mention the owner by name (signals small operation)
3. Cross-reference on Angi, Yellow Pages, Yelp to get phone and details
4. Build the demo. Then call.

PART 4 — HOSTING DEEP DIVE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

WHY HOSTING IS YOUR BEST LONG-TERM REVENUE:
Every client you sign adds $35/month forever with zero additional work
after setup. This is how recurring income compounds.

  10 clients  = $350/month
  20 clients  = $700/month
  30 clients  = $1,050/month
  40 clients  = $1,400/month

At 40 clients on hosting alone you're making $1,400/month doing nothing.
Stack retainers on top and the math gets very good.


OPTION A — RESELLER HOSTING (use once you have 3+ clients)
  Hostinger Reseller:   ~$30-40/month — host unlimited client sites
  SiteGround Reseller:  ~$50-85/month — better performance
  You charge clients:   $35/month each
  Math at 10 clients:   You pay $40, collect $350, NET $310/month


OPTION B — FREE HOSTING (use when starting out)
  Netlify free tier is fast, reliable, includes SSL, free.
  Problems with free hosting:
  - No revenue for you
  - Site lives in your account, messy at scale
  - No upsell path (hard to later charge $35/month)
  - No email hosting (can't do name@domain.com)
  - Bandwidth limits at scale

  Use Netlify to start. Move everyone to reseller once you have 3+ clients.


THE EMAIL HOOK — HOW TO GET CLIENTS OFF FREE HOSTING:
"One thing most business owners want eventually is a professional email —
like ken@rolandplumbing.com instead of Gmail. That's not something I can
do on free hosting, but if we move to managed hosting I can set that up
for you. It's $35 a month and covers everything — hosting, security,
backups, and the email."

A professional email is tangible. Every client understands it immediately.


DOMAIN MANAGEMENT:
  Buy through Namecheap or Cloudflare Registrar (~$10-15/year)
  Charge client $25/year
  Keep in your account, you manage it
  If they leave you, transfer it to them — make this clear upfront
  Never hold a domain hostage, it destroys your reputation


RECOMMENDED SETUP:
  Domains:  Namecheap ($10-15/year per domain)
  Hosting:  Hostinger Business Reseller (~$35/month, unlimited sites)
  Email:    Google Workspace ($6/month per user, client pays directly)
  Charge:   $35/month hosting + $25/year domain renewal

PART 5 — CRM & LEAD MANAGEMENT (KNOW THIS, DON'T PITCH IT EARLY)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

RULE: Don't bring up CRM unless they ask or they mention losing track of leads.
A solo tradesman who's been in business 26 years does not want to hear
about CRM workflows on a first call. It will lose you the deal.

BUT YOU SHOULD KNOW THIS SO YOU'RE READY:


WHEN THE PROBLEM APPEARS:
Client is getting 20+ inquiries a week and losing track, OR
Multiple people need to see leads (partner, family member, dispatcher), OR
They want to follow up systematically instead of randomly


THE CRM SPECTRUM:

Gmail + labels          Free      Solo, low volume. Completely fine to start.
Google Sheets           Free      Solo, wants a simple log of leads.
HubSpot Free            Free      Good pipeline view, general small biz.
Jobber                  $49-149/mo  BEST FOR TRADES. Built exactly for
                                    plumbers, HVAC, electricians. Scheduling,
                                    invoicing, job tracking, customer history,
                                    automatic follow-up texts. Recommend this
                                    when they outgrow Gmail.
HouseCall Pro           $65-250/mo  Similar to Jobber, more marketing features.
GoHighLevel             $97-297/mo  Full agency-grade, marketing automation.
                                    Overkill for most small trades guys.


FOR ROLAND SPECIFICALLY:
  Start: Contact form routes to Gmail. Done. This is fine.
  Month 3-6: If he mentions losing track of leads, introduce Jobber.
  Your fee: $300-500 setup + training. He pays Jobber directly monthly.


WHAT TO SAY IF IT COMES UP ON THE CALL:
"Honestly, for where you're starting, Gmail is fine. But if it ever
becomes a problem — if you're getting more leads than you can track —
there are simple tools that organize everything for you automatically.
I can set that up when you need it. It's not something you need on day one."


PROFESSIONAL EMAIL:
  Google Workspace: $6/month per user (client pays Google directly)
  Your setup fee: $75 one-time
  Takes: 30 minutes
  Pitch: "It's $6 a month straight to Google, I handle the setup for $75.
  Half an hour and you've got ken@rolandplumbing.com instead of Gmail."

PART 6 — THE SALES PROCESS (START TO FINISH)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

STEP 1 — PROSPECT
  Find on Google Maps. No website = your target.
  Cross-reference Angi, Yellow Pages, Yelp for phone and details.
  Look for: years in business, owner name in reviews, service area.

STEP 2 — BUILD THE DEMO FIRST (1-2 hours)
  Pull their info from public listings.
  Build on your template.
  Use branded placeholders where real photos would go.
  Deploy to a shareable URL.
  THEN call. Never call without the demo ready.

STEP 3 — THE CALL (see Part 7 for full script)
  Lead with the demo link.
  Get their name first.
  Ask what they actually do before you pitch.
  Close on a verbal yes.

STEP 4 — FOLLOW UP SAME DAY (within 1 hour of call)
  Text them:
  - Demo link
  - Contract
  - Intake form
  - Your Venmo/Zelle

STEP 5 — INTAKE FORM RECEIVED
  Build the real site within 1 week.
  One revision round included.
  Collect balance on approval.

STEP 6 — LAUNCH
  Set up hosting and domain.
  Submit to Google Search Console.
  Clean up Google Business Profile (if they bought it).

STEP 7 — POST-LAUNCH (2-3 weeks after)
  Call or text: "How's it going? Getting any calls from it?"
  This is where you pitch:
  - Monthly retainer if they haven't taken it
  - Print assets now that logo is done
  - Google Workspace email if they want it

STEP 8 — MONTH 3-6
  Check in. Any new services? Updated hours? Staff changes?
  Natural retainer conversation if not already on one.
  If they mention losing track of leads: introduce Jobber.

STEP 9 — MONTH 11-12
  Pitch the annual refresh.
  "It's been almost a year — let's make sure everything is current
  and accurate going into next year. I can do that for $350."
  Also: domain renewal, any new add-ons they're ready for.

PART 7 — FULL SALES SCRIPT (ROLAND / ANY TRADES CLIENT)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

BEFORE YOU DIAL:
  Demo link ready to text
  Contract ready to send
  Intake form ready to send
  Venmo/Zelle info ready


OPENING:
"Hey, is this Roland? My name's [name] — I'm a web developer based in
the area. Quick question before anything else — who am I actually speaking
with? I want to make sure I have the right person."

(Get their name. Use it from here on.)

"Perfect. So [name], I'm not calling to pitch you anything right now —
I actually already built something for you and I want two minutes of your
time to see if it's even worth a conversation."


THE HOOK:
"I was looking around York County for local trades businesses without a
website and your name came up. You've been doing this 26 years — that's
a serious reputation. But right now if someone in Red Lion or Shrewsbury
searches 'plumber near me' at 9pm when something breaks, they're not
finding you. They're finding whoever has a website. That's real business
going somewhere else every single week without you even knowing it."


THE DEMO:
"So I went ahead and put a demo together — your name, your phone number,
your service area, everything. Can I text you the link right now so you
can pull it up while we talk?"

(Text the link. Wait 20-30 seconds of silence. Let him look.)

"What do you think?"

(Shut up. Let him react first.)


HANDLING HIS REACTION:

If he likes it:
"Good — everything you're seeing I pulled from your Google listing and
public info, so some of it's going to be right and some of it's probably
off. I don't know if you do emergency calls, I don't know your exact
hours. I made educated guesses. The real build is where we sit down and
make it exactly yours. Does the direction feel right?"

If he nitpicks something:
"Write that down — that's exactly what we'd go through together once
we're working. Right now I just want to know if the overall feel works."

If he's skeptical about needing a website:
"That's fair. Can I ask — how are people finding you right now?"
(Whatever he says — referrals, word of mouth — follow with:)
"Those are people who already know you. A website is people who've never
heard of you but need exactly what you do tonight. Right now you're
invisible to all of them."

If he mentions he's busy:
"It sounds like you stay pretty busy — which is great. But I'd guess
there are people calling around when something breaks, and if they can't
reach you they're moving on to whoever comes up next on Google. A website
with a contact form captures those leads even when you're on a job and
can't answer. They fill it out, you call them back when you're free.
You don't lose the job just because you were busy."


LEARN ABOUT HIM (work in naturally, not rapid-fire):
"What does your typical week look like? What are you getting called for most?"
"Is it just you, or do you have guys working with you?"
"Do you cover the whole county or stick to certain areas?"

If he has multiple techs: more capacity = website pays for itself faster.
If he's solo and only does plumbing: simplify the site and pitch accordingly.


THE PRICE:
"Here's how I structure it. A full site — homepage, your service pages,
contact form — is $1,500. That's a one-time build, you own it outright.
If you want local pages on top of that, where you show up when people
search from Red Lion or Glen Rock or Shrewsbury specifically — that's
$2,000. Either way no ongoing fee from me."

(Pause. Let him respond.)

If he asks about hosting:
"Hosting is $35 a month — keeps the site live, keeps it secure, and
means you call me if anything goes wrong instead of tech support.
I handle everything on the backend."

If he hesitates on price:
"What are you charging for a service call these days? One new customer
a month from Google covers the whole site inside of 90 days."


NATURAL ADD-ON MOMENTS:

If he has no logo:
"I can put together a simple logo for you — clean text-based mark that
works on the site, on cards, everywhere. That's $150 and I'll have a
couple of options for you."

If he seems reluctant to fill out paperwork:
"Or if you'd rather just talk me through everything, I can handle all
the writing for you. That's $250 and you just answer my questions on
a quick call."

If he seems open and engaged:
"One other thing worth mentioning — your Google listing. You probably
have one but I'd bet the hours are off, maybe no photos, wrong category.
Fixing that is usually the fastest way to start getting calls. I can
clean it up while I'm building the site. That's $150 and it's one of
the best things you can do for local search."


THE CLOSE:
"I'm not asking you to decide everything today. But if this makes sense,
here's how it works — you sign a simple one-page agreement, put down
half upfront, fill out a quick info sheet I send you, and I have a draft
back to you within a week of getting your answers. Other half when you're
happy with it and it goes live. Pretty straightforward."

"Does that sound like something worth moving forward on?"


OBJECTION RESPONSES:

"I need to think about it."
"Totally fair. Can I follow up with you [specific day]?"
(Always get a specific day. No day = slow no.)

"I already have someone working on something."
"Good to know. If it falls through or you want a second opinion on what
they put together, keep my number. No pressure."

"I can't afford it right now."
"Is it a timing thing or is the price the issue? If it's timing I can
work with that — we can structure the payment around when it works."

"I don't think I need a website."
"You might be right. But you've got 26 years of reputation that nobody
can find online. Even if it gets you two or three new customers a year
it's paid for itself. What would one new customer a year be worth to you?"

"My nephew / someone I know does websites."
"Do they do it professionally? The difference is usually in the details —
SEO, mobile, making sure it actually shows up on Google. If they're
handling it, great. But if it's been on the to-do list for a while and
hasn't happened, that usually means it needs someone whose whole job it is."


ENDING THE CALL:

If he says yes:
"Perfect. I'll text you the contract and info sheet within the hour.
Once I have those back and the deposit I'll get started. You're going
to be glad you did this."

If he's not ready:
"No problem. I'll follow up [specific day]. The demo link I texted
stays live — feel free to poke around and show whoever you want."


SAME DAY FOLLOW-UP TEXT (within 1 hour):
"Hey [name], great talking with you. Here's everything:
Demo: [link]
Agreement: [link or attachment]
Info sheet: [link or attachment]
Venmo/Zelle: [your info]
Any questions just call or text. Looking forward to working with you."

PART 9 — CONTRACT SUMMARY (KEY TERMS TO ALWAYS INCLUDE)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

SCOPE:
  Define exactly what's included. Anything not listed is out of scope.
  "Additional pages, features, or redesigns are quoted separately."

REVISION ROUND:
  ONE round of revisions is included — AFTER the real site is built with
  client's actual content. The demo does not count as a draft.
  Additional rounds: $75/hour.

PAYMENT:
  50% deposit due at signing — work begins on receipt.
  50% balance due before launch.
  Deposit non-refundable if client cancels after work begins.

CONTENT DEADLINE:
  Client provides info, photos, and materials within 14 days of signing.
  Timeline extends if client delays.

PHOTOS:
  If client provides no photos, designer sources royalty-free stock at
  no extra cost. Client may supply real photos at any time — swapping
  them in after launch is covered under retainer or billed hourly.

HOSTING & DOMAIN:
  Client's responsibility and cost unless on managed hosting package.
  Domain is client's property — transferred to them if they ever leave.

OWNERSHIP:
  Client owns the site upon final payment.
  Designer retains right to display in portfolio.

POST-LAUNCH CHANGES:
  Covered under retainer ($100/month, 2 hours) or hourly ($75/hour).
  New features quoted separately.

STALL CLAUSE:
  If client goes 30 days without responding, project closes.
  Deposit is forfeited. Restart requires new deposit.

LIABILITY:
  No guarantees on search rankings or lead volume.
  No ongoing warranty beyond included revision round.

PART 10 — YEAR ONE ROADMAP
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

MONTH 1-2: PROVE THE MODEL
  Goal: Close Roland. Deliver fast. Get a testimonial.
  Metrics: 1 signed client, 1 launched site, 1 happy customer.
  Revenue target: $1,500-2,500

MONTH 2-3: REPEAT IT
  Goal: 2-3 more trades clients using same playbook.
  Start building a small portfolio. Get referrals — trades guys know
  each other. "Who else do you know that doesn't have a website?"
  Revenue target: $3,000-5,000

MONTH 3-4: ADD RESTAURANTS
  Goal: Use the restaurant template (John's Asian Cuisine style).
  Different pitch, same process. Different aesthetic.
  Revenue target: $4,000-6,000/month

MONTH 4-6: BUILD RECURRING BASE
  Goal: Get every client on managed hosting ($35/month).
  Start pitching retainers to active clients.
  Target: 8-10 clients on hosting = $280-350/month passive.
  Revenue target: $4,500-7,000/month

MONTH 6: EVALUATION POINT
  Are you consistently closing 2+ clients a month?
  Do you have $2,000-3,000/month in recurring revenue?
  If yes: this is your "consider leaving the job" signal.
  If no: keep building. Don't rush the jump.

MONTH 6-9: SYSTEMIZE
  Create templates for different industries.
  Start the config refactor (Tier 1+2) so builds get faster.
  Hire a virtual assistant for intake form follow-up if volume justifies.
  Target: 15+ clients, $4,000-5,000 MRR.

MONTH 9-12: SCALE
  Add upsells systematically: GBP cleanup, print, email setup.
  Start annual refresh conversations with month-3 clients.
  Consider raising base site price to $2,000 if booked 3+ weeks out.
  Target: $6,000-8,000/month total revenue.
